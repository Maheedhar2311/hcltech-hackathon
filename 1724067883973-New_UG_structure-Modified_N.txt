Introduction
This following draft guidelines are being presented in order to implement National Education Policy
(NEP) 2020 in an effective manner, taking into consideration various factors, such as flexible
curriculum, more stress on electives. This document is a draft of the revised curriculum of

Jalandhar (NITJ), with a perspective to execute the NEP 2020 in a phased manner.

Guidelines for DE/GE
Students have to complete 08 electives towards completion of B.Tech. Programme, with following
requirements:
Out of 08 Electives-
 At least 04 DE (from respective department)
 At least 02 GE (DE offered by all other Engineering/Sciences/Management departments)
 Remaining 02 electives to be opted from DE/GE. A student is also allowed to pursue a maximum of
02 generic elective courses in online mode through Massive Open Online Courses (MOOC)
platforms like NPTEL, Swayam, and other similar reputed platforms provided the same courses are
not being offered in the institute. The student will have the option to pick these courses from
Engineering, Sciences, Management, Humanities, History, Music, Art, Philosophy, Finance,
Psychology etc., However, prior approval from respective DUGC is required for all such courses.
The courses to be opted through MOOC platforms should meet either of the following criteria:
 Credits – Minimum 3 credits
 Duration – Minimum 8 weeks
 Contact hours – Minimum 32 hrs
The result of the candidate in respective online course would be mapped to an appropriate letter grade as
per the norms of the institute. Further, the letter grade awarded by the respective MOOCS course will be
considered as such. Absolute marks/percentage will be converted into appropriate letter grade using
absolute grading criterion if letter grade not awarded by the respective MOOCs courses. Further, the
students who will pursue GE in online mode through MOOCs platform will be required to obtain a Pass
Certificate for that particular course from the concerned MOOCs platform. The credits earned through
online courses will be counted towards major degree.

6-Months Internship
The Institute facilitates the students to pursue 6-months Internship during their 7th/8th semester (2023
batch onwards) subject to fulfilling the Institute guidelines. The purpose of providing this opportunity to
the students is to enhance their practical exposure and skill-sets in the relevant stream and eventually
their employability.
However, a maximum of 50% Students from the total class size will be allowed to avail the Internship in
a given semester.
 The students can opt for this internship opportunity either in the 7th or in the 8th semester provided
they do not have more than three backlogs in the preceding semesters.
 The Institute allows On-campus Internships for 6-months/1 semester duration to the 7th/ 8th semester
students. To facilitate this proposal, the TPO shall initiate the on-campus placement drive in the
mid of the 6th semester.
 Institute also allows Off-campus Internships at reputed research organizations e.g., ISRO, DRDO
etc., PSUs, and at Academic Institutes of repute.
 Course/project waiver of 13 Credits will be provided to a student in lieu of Internship in the 7th/8th
semester.
 No student will be allowed to apply to the respective department/TPO for Internship through
Off-campus mode after the last date of End-semester examination of 6th/7th Semester.
 If a student arranges off-campus 6-months Internship with some private company/industry, the same
has to be approved by Internship Advisory Committee (IAC) keeping in view whether the company
profile and intern role are aligned with the domain of the respective department and Internship
objectives.
Each department has an Internship Advisory Committee (IAC) appointed by the DUGC and comprising:
One senior faculty member as Chairperson, HoD, Final year faculty coordinator, and 01 faculty member
nominated by the DUGC, as members. The role of IAC would be as follows:
 Evaluate the Internship Notification Form received from the company and make a decision on
approving the internship.
 Approving off-campus internships keeping in view whether the company profile and intern role are
aligned with the domain of the respective department and Internship objectives

Flexibility in Evaluation System
The list of courses to be offered by a department in the subsequent semester is to be finalized during the
running semester by the concerned DUGC. All the requirements of the course structure are to be
considered before deciding the courses to be offered in a regular semester. The different components of
assessment for each offered course need to be mapped with Course Outcomes (COs) of respective
courses. The assessment model of each course shall be approved by the DUGC of the department and
shall be communicated to the students before the commencement of the course.

1. For Industrial Training and Summer Internship, evaluation will be based on the assessment by the
respective Industry (Profroma-1), report submitted by the student, and presentation and viva-voce
examination by the assessment committee formulated by the Head of the Department.
2. A student must secure minimum 30 % in End-Semester Examination and 40% cumulatively to clear
any course.
3. The Departments can evolve their own rubrics for the Mid-semester and End-semester evaluation of
Project work. The marks may be distributed among various components like selection of topic, problem
statement, literature review, methodology, oral and written presentation of the work done and performance
in viva-voce examination.
4. In case of the internal continuous assessment component, the course coordinators will have freedom to
devise appropriate assessment or evaluation methods of their respective courses. The course coordinators
may use the mix of quizzes, mini-projects, term paper, open-book tests, case studies or assignments or any
other challenging and innovative exercise that they deem fit and relevant for the respective course.
However, the assessment model of the course shall be approved by the DUGC of the department and shall
be communicated to the students before the commencement of the course.
5. Mid-semester and End-semester examinations will be coordinated by the Academic section for all the
courses of different undergraduate programmes. The dates for both examinations will be notified in the
institute Academic Calendar.
6. The maximum percentage of students to be awarded ‘S’ grade in a course may be around 5%.
7. The different components of assessment need to be mapped with Course Outcomes (COs) of respective
courses.
Every department shall ensure periodic revision of curriculum every 2 years to align it with the
technological advancements keeping in view the national and societal needs. Further, 10-20% of course
coverage may be delivered by Industry personnel in either online or offline mode.
